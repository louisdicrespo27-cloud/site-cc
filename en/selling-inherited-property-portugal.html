Selling inherited property in Portugal: the steps, from the United States

Updated November 16, 2026

You have inherited a house or an apartment in Portugal — alone, or with brothers, sisters or cousins — and you would like to sell it. Before a buyer can sign, several steps have to be completed in Portugal. This is where most sales stall: an heir nobody has heard from, a tax filing that was missed, a property that is not registered in the heirs' names.

This guide sets out, in order, what Portuguese law requires to sell inherited property when you live in the United States. It is written in particular for families of Portuguese descent who have inherited a property they have never managed themselves.

Who has to sign the sale

Until the estate is partitioned, the property belongs to all the heirs together, and no one owns a specific part of it. Rights relating to the estate can only be exercised jointly by all the heirs (Article 2091(1) of the Civil Code). In practice, every heir has to sign the sale — in person or through a power of attorney.

There are two other routes:

  • Partition first: the property is allocated to one heir, who then sells it alone;
  • Sell your share: an heir sells their share in the estate, without selling the property itself.

If an heir refuses or does not respond, see the section on undivided estates and the 2026 reform, which is not yet in force.

Step 1 — Establish who the heirs are

To sell, you need a document that identifies the heirs. In Portugal this is the declaration of heirs (habilitação de herdeiros), made before a notary or at the registry's inheritance desk (Balcão das Heranças). Documents from the United States, such as a death certificate, must be accompanied by a Portuguese translation; we tell you in advance whether an apostille is needed for each one.

Step 2 — The stamp duty filing

The estate must be declared to the Portuguese tax authority by the end of the third month after the month of death, and that deadline cannot be extended (Article 26 of the Stamp Duty Code). Transfers to a spouse, descendants and ascendants are exempt from the tax, but the filing is still required. Other heirs pay 10%.

A missed filing does more than hold up the sale: it brings penalties that add to the cost of the estate.

Step 3 — Register the property in the heirs' names

As a rule, a deed transferring real estate can only be signed if the property is definitively registered in the land registry in the name of the person transferring it (Article 9(1) of the Land Registry Code). Before selling, the acquisition by inheritance must therefore be registered in the heirs' names.

This is also the moment to check that the property is correctly described in the land registry and in the tax register. An area, a building or a boundary that does not match reality can delay a sale.

Step 4 — Prepare the sale

Each heir who sells needs a Portuguese NIF (tax number); see our guide on fiscal representation for what applies to residents of the United States. The file then brings together the documents required for any sale in Portugal, including the property's tax record (caderneta predial), the land registry certificate and the energy certificate. We check them before the promissory contract is signed, so that a problem does not surface a week before the deed.

The sale itself must be made by public deed or by an authenticated private document (Article 875 of the Civil Code). Heirs who live in the United States do not need to attend if they have given a valid power of attorney.

Capital gains tax in Portugal

The gain is not measured against what the person who died originally paid. For inherited property, the acquisition value is the value used — or that would have been used — for stamp duty (Article 45(1) of the Personal Income Tax Code), which is generally the property's official tax value. The lower that value is compared with the sale price, the larger the taxable gain.

For an heir who is not tax-resident in Portugal:

  • the gain is counted at 50% (Article 43(2) of the Personal Income Tax Code);
  • it is taxed at the flat rate of 28% (Article 72(1)).

The option to be taxed at the progressive rates that apply to residents (Article 72(15)) is available to residents of other EU and EEA states, not to residents of the United States. The gain is reported in Portugal in the annual income tax return. The sale is also relevant to your US tax return; we set out the Portuguese figures clearly so that your US accountant can work with them.

The mistakes that stall sales

  • Signing a promissory contract before the estate is ready. Few buyers will wait several months.
  • Overlooking an heir — for example, the child of an heir who has died, who inherits in their place.
  • Missing the stamp duty deadline.
  • Ignoring the land registry, or a description of the property that does not match reality.
  • Powers of attorney prepared separately by each heir, without coordination, and rejected on the day of the deed.
